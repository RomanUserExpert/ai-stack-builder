Design system / Why

Why it looks like this

The product reads like an inspection, not a sales sheet: it says what was examined, counts what it found and names the cost before the step. Every value in the system traces to a line of the owner's taste or to one of five attributes, written before anything was drawn. This page is read from 06-concept/concept.md and 06-concept/references.md; the specimens on it are live.

The owner's taste

Likes

Vercel

A clean interface without decoration: the status, the fact and the consequence stand side by side. Monochrome; colour only where it means something.

Likes

Figma

A dense working surface. A lot on screen, and it does not look overloaded, because the hierarchy is held by the grid and the type, not by colour.

Likes

Teams and Slack

Glass: translucent layers. The product's signature touch — noticeable, used sparingly.

Does not want.

— The palette an AI produces for the words “AI tool”: a dark ground, violet-blue neon gradients, glow.

— A marketplace showcase: Verified and Popular badges, stars, ratings, cover cards.

— Glass everywhere: translucent content, text on a blur, glass that eats contrast.

— A green tick as the main signal. It promises works, and we run nothing.

Five attributes this, not that

Each pair names the line of data it stands on, or says it is taste. A direction that broke a pair was not drawn.

1

An inspector's report, not a seller's badge

A verdict is facts side by side: what was examined, how long it took, the counts. Never a seal — no score, no rating, no green badge standing in for a sentence.

Data: EJ-2, believe that a clean result was earned

2

Dense and counted, not spacious and showcased

A row carries many small facts at once. Hierarchy comes from the grid and the type weight, not from colour or cards; a count sits by what it counts.

Data for the counts; taste for Figma

3

The consequence is visible before the step, not after it

The sentence that names a cost sits inside the control it applies to, above the button — never a toast afterwards, never Are you sure?

Data: EJ-1

4

Glass is a layer over the work, not the work itself

Glass only on a temporary surface — a dialog, the Filters popover. What a person reads to decide something is opaque, and text always sits on a sheet.

Taste; data only for where

5

Colour is a claim, held back

The interface is greyscale. Colour appears only where it states something the product knows: a Problem, a Note, what you can press, where you are.

Data: §5's refusal of badges; taste: Vercel
